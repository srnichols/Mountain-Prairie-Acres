How it is worked out

  • The sun's path at the lot (° N) in solar time, every 15 minutes on ten dates from May 15 to September 30, the months both species are in leaf. June 21 has hours of sun more than 3° above the horizon.
  • Every plant is an ellipsoid of its managed width and height from the plan: mulberry ft wide and – ft up, hazelnut ft wide to about ft, berries ft wide to about ft. A hazelnut is tested against every other crown within 120 ft along the row, from its own middle.
  • A beam loses about of its remaining light for each foot of crown it crosses, so about four-fifths is gone over 20 ft. The sun counts in proportion to its height, the beam on level ground. No clouds, no trees outside the strip, and mature size: in the first eight to ten years the hazelnuts outgrow the mulberries and get full sun.
  • This is a planning estimate, not a measurement. Differences of a few points are within its error. The gap between the two ways of growing the mulberries is the result to trust.

What was planted

The planted row is one line, 5 ft from the west fence. It starts with two hazelnuts, then a red mulberry, and that repeats every 6 ft. It starts even with the planned northwest corner of the west gothic greenhouse and runs north until the plants ran out. All red mulberries and all hazelnuts are planted. The hazelnuts ran out first, so the north end has empty hazel places and the mulberries continue on their 18 ft spacing.

The berry plants go in front of that line, one in each gap, except one bush left out about halfway between each pair of orchard rows so you can walk through. The bushes those openings displace continue north. They are not planted yet. The tractor alley is ft clear from the berry row to the orchard limbs.
