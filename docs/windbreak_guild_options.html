Light on the hazelnuts

Hazelnuts set a crop in the sun, and the guild row puts them under and beside the mulberry crowns.   • Left to spread, the guild row costs the hazelnuts much more than two rows do, because the hazelnuts sit under the crowns instead of ft away. Two rows lose sun too, but they start from a better place.
  • The berry row keeps most of the season's sun in every pattern, so Josta and the other berries are not the problem.

How it is worked out

  • The sun's path at the lot (° N) in solar time, every 15 minutes on ten dates from May 15 to September 30, the months both species are in leaf. June 21 has hours of sun more than 3° above the horizon.
  • Every plant is an ellipsoid of its managed width and height from the plan: mulberry ft wide and – ft up, hazelnut ft wide to about ft, berries ft wide to about ft. A hazelnut is tested against every other crown within 120 ft along the row, from its own middle.
  • A beam loses about of its remaining light for each foot of crown it crosses, so about four-fifths is gone over 20 ft. The sun counts in proportion to its height, the beam on level ground. No clouds, no trees outside the strip, and mature size: in the first eight to ten years the hazelnuts outgrow the mulberries and get full sun.
  • This is a planning estimate, not a measurement. Differences of a few points are within its error. The gap between the two ways of growing the mulberries is the result to trust.

Which pattern to pick

B, two hazelnuts between mulberries

It is the version you described, it keeps most of both the mulberries and the hazelnuts you have, and with firm pruning it leaves the hazelnuts within a few points of today's light. It is the one the mock-up opens on.

C, if the hazelnuts are the crop you want

The most light on the hazelnuts, and the best chance of keeping some when a mulberry gets away from you. It needs the fewest mulberries, against the you have, but hazelnuts against the you have, and it leaves the most mulberries spare for another fence.

A, only for the tallest wall

It keeps the mulberries about as close as they are today and puts a hazelnut between each pair, the densest and tallest barrier. It needs mulberries against the you have. The hazelnuts get the least light, and a spreading mulberry would shade them out.

Whichever you pick, the tractor alley is the same ft clear instead of , and the strip ends ft from the fence instead of . The pattern changes what grows in the strip, not how much room it takes.

If you adopt one: what would change

  • The data. WINDBREAK in data/orchard.json holds two rows 15 ft apart. It would become a guild row and a berry row, and C1_GUILDS would change if the berries move out of the orchard. Then run python tools\property_data.py build.
  • The model. web/orchard_model.js (windbreakLayout, laneGeometry, guildLayout) assumes two single-plant rows and refuses rows closer than the shade gap. A row that mixes two kinds needs a new shape.
  • The pages. The windbreak figures, table, and notes on the orchard pollination grid, and the tests that check them.
  • The water plan. Zones 7, 8, and 9 in data/water_harvesting.json name the hazelnut row, the walking lane, and the C1 guilds.
  • The income page. docs/income_plan.js counts mulberries and hazelnuts.
  • These two pages. They would be retired once the plan itself draws the guild row.
