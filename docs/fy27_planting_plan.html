Where they go

Draft placement, rows listed south to north as in the planting map. C1 is the west end of each row and C13 the east end by the driveway; every other row is staggered half a spot. Where it works, same-cultivar trees sit side by side for signage and easy picking: the Williams' Pride trio and two of the three Freedom. Pollination check

Using the pollination grid's rules: partners are other cultivars within 50 ft that bloom in the same or an adjacent period and are compatible (apples: diploid donors whose flowering group is within one of the tree's own; cherries and pears: the bloom charts on the grid page). Living trees and the other planned trees count.

Season fit

Bands are relative to a cool northern apple harvest: Early before about Sept 10, Mid about Sept 10 to Oct 5, Late about Oct 5 to 25, Very late after Oct 25 (see the guide). Unidentified apples are not banded.

Why these picks

The site

  • About 4,750 ft, nearly flat; Simonton loam with soil pH about 6.2; 12 to 16 in of precipitation, with July and August almost dry (see the water-harvesting plan).
  • NRCS lists only 60 to 100 frost-free days for this soil's setting. The four NOAA stations serving Elmore County share an 80-day season, and the longest single station allows 160 days; the nearest similar-elevation station (Hill City, 5,100 ft) has a median season of 111 days. Best estimate here: 70 to 110 days and USDA zone 5a to 5b. Sources disagree and the USDA map was not checked for this exact point, so treat these as estimates, not readings.
  • In general, bloom-frost risk runs worst to best from apricot to peach, sweet cherry, pear, and apple (a general horticultural ranking, not something measured here). That is why apples carry the plan and the three replacements are chosen for hardiness: late bloom or frost-tolerant buds.

What a U-pick orchard needs

  • Fruit people eat off the tree, a harvest that stretches over weeks (the orchard had four early apples), trees small enough to pick without ladders, and few sprays. That points to semi-dwarf trees and disease-resistant cultivars.
  • Sweet cherries and European pears are the harder U-pick crops (birds and splitting; pears are picked hard and ripen off the tree), so the single replacement trees are a modest bet, not a block.

Bacterial canker in the stone fruit

Suggestions not followed

  • Encore peach topped the first round of research for being the latest ripener. It ripens in fall, after Elberta, which is already a gamble here, so Intrepid replaces it.
  • Gisela 6 cherry rootstock was suggested for Black Gold. The Intermountain Tree Fruit Production Guide lists Gisela 6 as the most bacterial-canker-susceptible rootstock and Mazzard as the least, so the plan says Mazzard.

Ripening watch: apples you already have

These apples ripen very late, after about Oct 25 in a cool northern climate. With 70 to 110 frost-free days they may not ripen reliably here. Watch them for the first few crops; any that cannot ripen before the hard freeze can be top-worked (grafted over) to an early or mid-season cultivar.

Braeburn at R2-C2 belongs on this watch list too: its band is Late, but it is disease-susceptible and wants a long warm fall. Golden Delicious, Idared, Red Delicious, and Mutsu are less likely to be a problem: the Intermountain Tree Fruit Production Guide's apple table, written for Utah, lists them as cold hardy with September to October harvests. Prairie has a shorter season than most of Utah, so treat that as encouraging, not proof.

Sources and caveats

  • Site: NRCS Web Soil Survey report (soil pH, frost-free period); US Growers Guide: Planting zones in Elmore County, Idaho (zones 5a to 7a; last spring freeze May 1 to June 16 and first fall freeze Sept 4 to Oct 8 across four NOAA stations); Bield: Elmore County frost dates (Hill City 1 W, 5,100 ft: median last frost May 30, first fall frost Sept 18, 111 days, zone 5b); University of Idaho Extension: backyard tree fruit in harsher climates (apples, apricots, cherries, pears, and plums can be grown in short-season zones with the right cultivars).
  • Cherries: Oregon State Extension: sweet cherry compatibility and bloom timing chart (Black Gold in pollination period IV; Bing and Stella in II; Lapins in I); Intermountain Tree Fruit Production Guide: bacterial canker (rootstocks and copper sprays).
  • Apples: Intermountain Tree Fruit Production Guide: apple varieties for Utah (harvest times and hardiness for Golden Delicious, Idared, Red Delicious, Mutsu, and Haralson).
  • Cultivar facts, ratings, and ripening dates are in each profile's sources in the cultivar guide.
