Crop and producer income

What each part of the lot could add to a homestead business · planning allowances, not a promise · October 28, 2026

This page prices the producers already on the place, the ones you named, and a few the inventory already implies. The number is gross sales: pounds times a farmgate price times the share you can actually sell. Feed, pots, fuel, the drive to Mountain Home or Boise, and your time are not subtracted. A total of several thousand dollars is a delivery route or a shipped product. The gate in Prairie will not clear it.

Hard cider is not in the total. Selling it needs a license. Jam and syrup are the same fruit at a higher price, not a second crop, so they are not added on top of the berry and elderberry lines.

Change the assumptions

Counts, prices, and the sold share are yours to move. Changes stay in this browser. They are not written back to the property records.

Left out on purpose

  • Hard cider, wine, and any other alcohol.
  • Jam, jelly, and syrup as extra income on top of the fruit they are made from.
  • The workshop. It is a cost until it does custom work, and that work is not a crop.
  • Agritourism. The lot is too remote, and the season is too short, to count on visitors.

Prices are 2026 direct-sale allowances for a small Idaho flock and a farmstand, not Boise farmers-market peaks and not wholesale. Orchard pounds match the trees planted in October 2026: about 40 dessert apples, 24 pears, 31 stone-fruit trees, and the cider and crab trees pressed separately. Greenhouse dates come from the tunnel and pit pages. Hedge counts come from the inventory: 34 Jefferson hazelnuts, 25 red mulberries, 19 elderberries.
