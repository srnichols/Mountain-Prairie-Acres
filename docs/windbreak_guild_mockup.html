How it is laid out

The mulberries stay ft from the fence, where they are in the plan today. The hazelnuts stand in the same row, between each pair of mulberries, with every place ft from the next along the row, so each hazelnut is ft from the trunk of the mulberry beside it and one guild is ft of fence. A row of small berries stands ft from the fence, with a ft pick lane between it and the hazelnuts. Everything now ends ft from the fence instead of .

What you gain

  • A wider alley. The tractor alley is ft clear instead of ( ft against ft if every plant reaches its full width). About sq ft is freed along the ft of fence, and all of it goes to the alley: the orchard trees are in the ground, so the orchard does not move.
  • No dead lane. The ft walking lane, and the ft shade gap that called for it, become a ft pick lane.
  • One barrier, dense from the ground up. The hazelnuts fill the open space under the mulberry crowns, so the row blocks wind near the ground as well as above it. Red mulberry grown in the open tends to have low limbs, and the hazelnuts cover the base while you lift them.
  • A home for the berries. All berry bushes planned around the C1 trees move to the lee of the row, and the C1 trees no longer need guilds around them. Each kind stands in a run, so the honeyberries are one group and a net covers a run. Only the gooseberries are likely to have thorns (their cultivar is not recorded); the rest are thornless, so the row is easy to pick.

What it costs

  • Hazelnut light depends on the mulberry crowns. With the mulberries kept as the plan assumes (), the hazelnuts get of the season's direct sun now and in pattern . If the mulberries are left to spread (), it is now and here. The guild row works only with firm pruning: one trunk, limbs from ft, and the east side cut back to ft from the trunk. How the light is worked out.
  • Fewer mulberries, or fewer hazelnuts. This pattern plants mulberries ( now; ) and hazelnuts ( now; ).
  • Tighter work space. With no walking lane, mulberries are pruned and hazelnuts picked from the ft pick lane or from the alley side. That takes a person and a cart, not a mower.
  • Shade and snow on the berries. The row throws afternoon shade on them: about of the season's sun reaches the berry row. West winds drop their drifts just east of the row, over the berries and the alley, as the water plan already expects for the hazelnut row.

What else it touches

  • The water plan. Zone 9 makes the walking lane a sunken wood-chip path between the two rows; here that job moves to the pick lane. Zone 8's water bars turn water west into the hazelnut row, which becomes the guild row, and zone 7's drifts land on the berry row. See the water-harvesting plan.
  • The income page. It counts mulberries and hazelnuts; see crop and producer income.
  • The wind. No wind has been measured at the lot, and the stations disagree about winter wind direction. A west windbreak, in either layout, is a bet that the winter wind blows from the west; the site baselines say to wait for a year of weather-station readings.

Decisions for you

  • Which pattern. A keeps the most mulberries but gives the hazelnuts the least light. C gives the hazelnuts the most light and uses the fewest mulberries. B sits between. Choose above and the figures redraw.
  • Where the berries go. The figures move all of them to the front row. Splitting them, some here and some around the C1 trees, leaves places open in the row; keep the honeyberries together wherever they go. Comfrey, rhubarb, and the brambles are left out of the row.
  • How firmly you will prune. The numbers above assume the plan's pruning. A mulberry left to spread costs the hazelnuts far more in the guild row than in two rows.
