Site Climate: Frost, Temperature, and Daylight

Early and late frost at both ends of the season, with the chance of a freeze by any date, plus monthly freeze days, average highs and lows, and day length. Measured at the Prairie SNOTEL, 2.4 miles away: a typical year has 90 frost-free days (1 year in 10 has fewer than 74), and the cold nights put the orchard in zone 5b.

Next: work through the "Still to Check" list on the page: log bloom dates, put a minimum thermometer in the orchard (and one in the low northwest corner) from March to October to see how cold air behaves and how the lot compares with the station, find out which way the wind blows, and confirm the county's snow load and frost depth. Project Log

  • Oct 19, 2026: Added a site baselines page and data: reference numbers from the nearest stations, models, and county documents that the climate page does not hold. The snowpack at the Prairie SNOTEL typically peaks at 5.8 inches of water around Feb 20 and melts out about Mar 26, and 2026 peaked at 1.1 inches, the second lowest in 40 years. A 25-year, 24-hour storm drops 2.55 inches at the lot (NOAA Atlas 14). Counted from hourly readings, a typical year has 2,104 degree days above 50 °F and a typical winter has 872 chill hours between 32 and 45 °F. Wind comes from a model at the lot and two measured stations, and they disagree: the model and Mountain Home Air Force Base put the most common winter wind in the east, while Pine Turnoff, the station closest in height, puts it in the west, so the water plan's west-wind assumption is unconfirmed. The county puts Prairie in the 120 lb/sq ft ground snow load zone, not the 30 lb/sq ft zone that covers the rest of the county, and its permit application asks for an engineer's report above about 5,000 ft; the lot is at about 4,750 ft, so confirm with the county. Four checks were added to the climate page's "Still to Check" list: wind direction, the county's snow load, the county's frost depth wording, and a year of weather station readings against these numbers. None of these numbers is a reading on the lot. Left out: smoke, soil temperature and moisture (the weather station's soil sensors will measure them), and evapotranspiration (already in the water plan).
  • Oct 18, 2026: Changed the 2027 stone-fruit plan to 4 plums, 2 apricots, and 4 peaches in the same ten spots in R11 and R12.
