Site Climate: Frost, Temperature, and Daylight

Early and late frost at both ends of the season, with the chance of a freeze by any date, plus monthly freeze days, average highs and lows, and day length. Measured at the Prairie SNOTEL, 2.4 miles away: a typical year has 90 frost-free days (1 year in 10 has fewer than 74), and the cold nights put the orchard in zone 5b.

Next: work through the "Still to Check" list on the page: log bloom dates, put a minimum thermometer in the orchard (and one in the low northwest corner) from March to October to see how cold air behaves and how the lot compares with the station, find out which way the wind blows, and confirm what the county's 30 inch frost depth means for a footing. The official USDA zone check and the county snow load check are done: the map reads 6a for the lot's cell, against 5b from the station's readings, and the county required a neighbor's building to be designed for 120 lb/sq ft, so that is the design snow load.

Workshop Pole Barn: 32 × 48 ft, Planned for 2028

A 32 × 48 ft gambrel pole barn with an RV bay sized for the owner's 2024 Rockwood Mini Lite 2517S camper (a 14 ft first-story wall, a 14 × 12 ft overhead door, and a ridge under the county's 27 ft limit), a workshop, and a storage loft with a hoist that reaches past a landing deck, three windows in each long wall and one in the west loft wall, on the level pad of imported fill north of the pump house. Every drawing, count, and price comes from one design model, so the length sets the post bays, the truss count, the drawings, and the prices. Six pages: an overview with the site fit and Elmore County's height, size, permit, and pole barn guide rules, a spec sheet, plans, elevations, the framing in perspective, and the girt framing of all four walls, sections with seven construction details, a truss package to send to a truss plant (the open room and its collar tie, the bracing and the heel, the loads, and the special trusses), and a 21-phase bill of materials. The design uses 120 psf ground snow, the load the county required of a neighbor's building, and 115 mph wind, so the building is engineered. It shows the fill as almost 2 ft thick on the north edge and 6 in to 1 ft on the south, and puts the all-in budget at about $103,600 to $176,700 (about $137,600 at the midpoint).

Next: ask Elmore County in writing how it measures the building's height (20′-4″ at mid-gable against a 24 ft accessory limit; 15′-1″ to the eave and 26′-4″ to the roof peak from the pad against the pole barn guide's usual 14 ft wall and 27 ft peak), whether the front-yard size cap applies, and whether this is an accessory building or an agricultural one, which depends on what it will hold; measure the camper as parked; shoot the pad's elevation and have the fill checked; get stamped truss and foundation drawings for 120 psf (the truss package page is the brief to send a truss plant); and get quotes on the trusses, the column package, the steel, the overhead door, and the concrete.
