The Building in Brief

RV garage, workshop, and storage loft. What the building really holds decides its permit path: see County rules
Footprint

Where It Sits

The workshop goes on the graded pad north of the pump house and the containers, with its east door lined up with the road-mix path and the RV path. Its footprint is in data/site.json at 32 × 48 ft, centered on the pad.

Notes on the pad and the foundation

  • Fill and compaction. Ask the engineer whether the fill was placed in compacted lifts. If that is not documented, plan to compact and test it, or to deepen the gravel base, before the slab goes on it.
  • The pier holes go through the fill. The 60 in is measured from the pad surface, so the north row's holes pass through about 2 ft of fill and end about 3 ft into native soil, and the south row's end about 4 to 4½ ft into it. Each pier pad should bear on undisturbed native soil, or on fill the engineer accepts. Detail 3 and Section A draw this.
  • The north edge. The fill is thickest along the north edge, where the pad reaches only 5 ft beyond the wall (4 ft past each end). Check that the edge is firm and not slumping, and keep the north wall's piers and the slab edge well inside it.
  • The pad surface. The lidar predates the pad, so shoot the finished surface with a level and record it here. The slab, foam, and gravel stack up about 13 in above it, so the floor sits about 1′-1″ above the pad.

County Rules and What They Mean Here

From Elmore County's published Zoning and Development Ordinance (Title 7, chapters 1 and 2, adopted May 2018, amended 2023 and 2024), the building regulations (Title 9), and the county's Notice of Proposed New Agricultural Building, read in October 2026 at elmorecounty.org. The ordinance is being amended (case ZOA-2026-01, to match the updated Comprehensive Plan; the staff report for the October 22, 2026 hearing recommends continuing the review to November 18, 2026). The draft keeps the height table and the accessory- and agricultural-structure standards cited below, but it reorganizes chapter 2, so section numbers may change. This is a planning summary, not legal advice and not the county's answer: read the current text and get each answer in writing.

Which permit path is this building on?

The county sorts a building by what it is used for, not by its name.

  • Accessory building: the design as drawn. An RV garage, a workshop, and a storage loft. The house comes first, and the height, size, and matching rules above apply. It needs a building permit, plan review, and inspections.
  • Agricultural building. A barn for orchard equipment, a tractor, bins, and harvest storage. It is exempt from the building code, but not from zoning: it needs zoning approval before construction and the county's agricultural exemption in place of a building permit, and the district's setbacks and height still apply. It must stand on a farm (5 acres or more in agricultural use, tied to the assessor's tax categories), so ask whether the parcel qualifies. The owner signs a notarized statement, recorded with the county, that the building is used solely for agriculture.

The two cannot be mixed: the definition says solely. With the RV inside, the building is on the first path, and a building later put to another use can be required to obtain every permit and prove it meets the code. If the plan changes to a farm barn, the package should be redrawn for that use (a different name, no camper clearances), and the application should describe what the building will hold.

Before Any Site Work

  1. Call the Elmore County Land Use and Building Department (208-587-2130, extension 6500): confirm the ground snow load, wind speed, frost depth, and exposure for this parcel, and which rules apply at about 4,750 ft.
  2. Settle the permit path with the county, in writing (see County rules): the parcel's zoning district (some of the rules apply only in residential districts); whether this is an accessory building or an agricultural building, which follows from what it will really hold; how the height is measured, from what grade, and how a gambrel is averaged; the front-yard size cap; and whether the house must be permitted first.
  3. Get stamped truss drawings from a pre-fab truss company, with the loft stair opening, the loft doors, and the hoist-beam loads on them.
  4. Get stamped foundation and structural plans from an Idaho-licensed engineer, including what to do about the pad's fill.
  5. Pull the building permit.
  6. Call 811 before any digging. The buried 1.5 in water line is about 11 ft south of the south wall and the west-fence line about 16 ft west.
  7. Settle the power source and run the sub-feed conduit before backfill.
  8. Shoot the pad's elevation with a level and get the fill checked before the footings.

Open Questions

  1. What will the building really hold? That decides the permit path (see County rules). An RV garage and a workshop make it an accessory building, which is what these drawings show. A barn for orchard equipment and harvest could be an agricultural building. It cannot be both.
  2. Which snow load applies? The county's 120 psf zone is used here; the permit page ties it to buildings above about 5,000 ft.
  3. Where does the power come from? The pump house with its solar inverters and batteries, or the house? A 200 A panel is a panel rating, not a supply: check that the source can run the welder (50 A), the compressor (30 A), and the mini-split together.
  4. What is the hoist for? Lifting from the deck needs only the 3 ft beam. Lifting from the ground has to clear the 10 ft deck, so the beam would reach about 12 ft out and the engineer would size it and its back-span.
  5. How far does the slab slope? The slab slopes toward the east door. At ¼ in per ft over 48 ft that is 12 in, which is a lot. Slope only the 12 to 16 ft nearest the door, or use ⅛ in per ft; decide with the concrete subcontractor.
  6. Stove, condenser, and panel. The wood stove's spot is not chosen. The mini-split condenser goes on the west or the south wall.
  7. The winter wind. The door is on the east end on the idea that a prevailing west wind hits the closed back wall. If the winter wind comes from the east instead, the door and the deck take it and the drifts pile up there. The weather station's first year should settle it.
  8. Camper height. The drawings use an 11′-10″ camper roof, which leaves 2′-2″ under the door head and 3′-4″ under the loft. Measure yours.
