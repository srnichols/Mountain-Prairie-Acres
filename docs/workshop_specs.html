Workshop Pole Barn: Spec Quick Reference

Window pre-planning

  • Each window is centered between two posts, so no post is moved. At each opening cut the girts, set a doubled 2×6 header on jack framing, and add a 2×6 sill and blocking for the J-trim.
  • Flash every window the same way: sill pan first, then the jamb tape, then the head tape, lapped under the housewrap above and over it below. The rainscreen cavity stays open behind the trim, and the steel is finished around each window with J-trim and a head flashing.

Loft door and deck pre-planning

  • Tell the truss plant the loft door rough opening on the east gable: about 5′-6″ wide × 6′-6″ tall, centered, bottom at the loft floor.
  • Header above the loft doors: (2) 2×10 LVL, or as the engineer says.
  • Hoist beam included: a cantilevered steel I-beam (or doubled LVL) projecting about 3 ft beyond the loft doors, sized by the engineer for a 500–1,000 lb working load. Anchor it to the truss top chord and to blocking inside the loft, with an eye plate or trolley track for the chain fall.
  • Deck piers: 4 concrete piers below frost (60 in), 18 in Ø tubes with a belled footing. Anchor the 6×6 posts with post bases. The posts stand on the door-jamb lines, so they do not narrow the door.
  • The deck is free-standing (no ledger on the building), which protects the rainscreen and the siding from leaks.
  • The four posts need lateral bracing (14 ft wide and 16 ft tall is tall and narrow): diagonal knee braces or X-bracing per the engineer.
  • Decking: PT, composite, or Trex, the owner's choice, with stainless or hot-dip galvanized fasteners.
  • No awning over the deck. No exterior stairs to grade: the deck is reached from the loft only.

What NOT to do

  • ❌ Steel screwed straight to the OSB (no rainscreen): a sure condensation failure
  • ❌ 15 or 30 lb felt behind the wall steel: it tears and traps moisture
  • ❌ Poly vapor barrier outside the OSB: it traps moisture and rots the sheathing
  • ❌ Foil-faced bubble wrap as the WRB: it is not a real WRB
  • ❌ Ice and water shield on the walls: a vapor trap, the wrong tool
  • ❌ Wood posts buried in the dirt at this snow load: use brackets on piers
  • ❌ Skipping the H2.5A hurricane ties: 115 mph wind needs them
  • ❌ Skipping the rebar in the slab: 5 in of concrete under stored loads will crack
  • ❌ Pouring the slab or setting footings on uncompacted fill without the engineer's say-so
